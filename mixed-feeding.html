Mixed feeding: how to keep track of breast, bottle and pumping.

When some feeds are from the breast and some from a bottle, it's easy to lose track of what your baby had and when. Here's the NHS guidance, and how to log it all in one place.

What the NHS suggests

  • What it isMixed feeding combines breastfeeding with bottles of expressed breast milk, formula, or both. It can help you share feeds with a partner or family.
  • Get comfortable with breastfeeding firstIf possible, settle into breastfeeding before adding bottles. The NHS suggests 6 to 8 weeks of breastfeeding first.
  • Protect your milk supplyExpress regularly between bottle feeds to help keep your supply up.
  • Make bottles feel familiarUse a slow-flow teat, offer the first bottle when your baby is calm, and use the positions you'd use to breastfeed.
  • Cut down graduallyIf you're reducing breastfeeds, do it slowly to lower the risk of mastitis.
  • SupportTalk to your GP, midwife or health visitor, or call the National Breastfeeding Helpline on 0300 100 0212, open 24 hours a day.

Source: NHS Best Start in Life, Introduction to mixed feeding

Tracking it with Tiny Logger

  1. Breastfeeds with a timerTime the left and right side, and pause and resume when you need to.
  2. Bottles of breast milk or formulaLog what was in the bottle and how much your baby took.
  3. PumpingRecord each session with the side and how much you expressed, so you can see your supply over time.

This is not medical advice

Tiny Logger helps you keep records. It does not diagnose. If you're worried about your baby, talk to your midwife, health visitor or GP, call NHS 111, or in an emergency call 999.
